# The firefly and the light in the window

*A bedtime story — Book Five, following "The firefly and the cricket who lost his voice"*

---

That summer, at the top of the hill, something happened that had never happened to the meadow before.

A light came on.

It wasn't the moon and it wasn't a star. It was a window: yellow and square, in a house that hadn't been there before. And it stayed on all night.

The fireflies stopped in mid-air to look at it.

«How pretty,» said one of the little ones.

But the next night the window was still on. And the night after that. And the night after that.

And the meadow began to change colour.

It didn't go dark: it went *pale*. The grass wasn't black any more, it was grey. The brook didn't glitter, it just glinted. And the fireflies — every one of them, even the strongest — flew and flew, and couldn't be seen.

They were still lit. But next to that window their light was nothing at all.

One night a snail got lost two steps from her own house. Bea flew round her for half an hour making all the light she had, and the snail didn't even see her.

Bea went back to the oak and landed beside Nina.

«Nina,» she said. «What's the use of lighting up, if nobody can see us?»

And Nina — who had an answer for everything, who always knew what to say — opened her mouth and said nothing.

It was the first time in her whole life that she didn't know.

They both sat quietly, looking at the yellow window.

Then something moved in the grass. It was Rocco, who had climbed all the way up there slowly, the way he did now.

He couldn't speak. Nothing had come out of him for two summers.

But he raised one leg, and pointed at the hill.

«Up there?» said Nina.

Rocco said yes with his antennae.

«But no firefly has ever left the meadow.»

Rocco just looked at her, with those old eyes, and Nina understood that this was exactly the point.

She left that same night.

She flew over the brook, then over the hedge, then over the long grass she had never crossed. The window got bigger and yellower, and at a certain point Nina could no longer see the meadow behind her.

She was frightened. But she kept going.

When she reached the glass she landed, and looked in.

There was a room. In the room there was a bed. And in the bed there was somebody small, wide awake, with the covers pulled up to their nose and their eyes open.

Beside the bed there was a lamp, switched on.

Nina clung to the glass without understanding.

Then she understood.

*Ah*, she thought. *But this I know about.*

*This I know all about.*

Because that lamp wasn't on out of spite, and it wasn't on against the meadow.

It was on because on the other side of the glass, somebody was afraid of the dark.

And so Nina did the only thing she had ever known how to do.

She lit up.

Not hard. Softly: a little golden warm light, resting on the glass, breathing.

And she was seen.

The covers came down. Nobody called out, nobody was frightened. That gold dot on the glass was looked at for a very long time.

Then a hand reached out — slowly, the way you do when you're still a bit afraid, but not very —

and turned off the lamp.

And something happened.

The meadow, down at the bottom, lit up all at once.

Two hundred fireflies, who had been there all along and had never once gone out, appeared in the grass in an instant, like a city. The brook began to shine again. The oak went black again.

And from the window something was seen that had never been seen, because to see it you had to make it dark.

Against the glass, a finger's width away, was Nina.

And they looked at each other.

From that night the meadow knew something it hadn't known before: that there was somebody up there.

And on the other side of the glass, now, somebody knew there was somebody down here.

And every evening, when the light in the window went out, the whole meadow lit up.

It had become a way of saying goodnight.

You've got a lamp beside your bed too, my love.

And it's absolutely fine to keep it on, if you need it tonight: nobody down here takes it badly.

But if one time you feel up to turning it off, try looking outside.

Because some things have always been there, and they're only waiting for you to make a bit of dark so they can be seen.

Goodnight. 🌙✨
