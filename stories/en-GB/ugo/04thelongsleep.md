# The long sleep

*A bedtime story — Book Four, following "The noise in the ceiling"*

---

In November the mosquitoes ran out.

Not all at once: one evening there were ten at Breath's window, then five, then a single one whining softly, as if it were a bit cold too. Then none.

And Ugo knew what that meant.

The long sleep was coming.

Every bat has it. When it gets cold and there's nothing left to eat, the body starts slowing down by itself: the heart beats slowly, then more slowly, then so slowly you can hardly hear it; the wings fold up like an umbrella; and you sleep. Not for a night, not for a week. You sleep until March.

There's no arguing with the long sleep. It comes the way snow comes: it doesn't ask whether you agree.

But that year Ugo had a problem he had never had before.

The three knocks.

Every evening, from below, Breath knocked. And every evening Ugo answered. It had become their thing, like Teaspoon's *tink tink tink*, like Heavy Step's two stairs at a time.

If Ugo slept until March, who would answer?

Breath would knock, and the ceiling would stay silent. One evening, two, ten. And in the end Breath would think the one in the ceiling had gone away.

Ugo would have liked to explain. But how do you explain a thing like that, with three knocks?

On the last evening he tried anyway. When he heard the knocking, he answered. Then he knocked three more times, very slowly, with long silences in between: the way somebody knocks when they're terribly sleepy. It was the closest thing to *goodnight, see you in spring* that a bat could say with one claw.

Below, silence. Who knows whether Breath had understood.

Then Ugo tried to stay awake.

He counted the beams. He counted the tiles. He hung from one foot only, just to be uncomfortable. He went over all the shapes of the meadow in his head: the round oak, the thick hedge, the smooth brook.

He got as far as the brook. Then he didn't get anywhere any more.

He was asleep.

That winter it snowed. The snow settled on the roof, white and silent, and under the tiles the cold grew so deep it was like a room of its own.

Ugo, hanging from his beam, wrapped in his wings, heard nothing. His heart beat every now and then, like somebody remembering something and then forgetting it again.

And underneath him, every evening, somebody knocked three times.

*Knock. Knock. Knock.*

And nobody answered.

Some evenings the knocks were loud, almost cross. Some evenings they were small, like a question asked under your breath. Some evenings there was only one, and then nothing more. But the next evening there were three again.

All winter long.

In March, one evening, Ugo opened an eye.

His heart started again slowly, like a clock somebody winds one turn at a time. His wings were stiff, his legs were numb, and he was hungry enough to eat every mosquito on the hill — though there weren't any yet.

He dragged himself towards the hole. *Scritch. Scritch-scritch.*

And before he had even got to the *tick*, from below:

*Knock. Knock. Knock.*

Straight away. As if somebody had been waiting there with their hand already raised.

Ugo stopped. He stretched out his claw, still a little crooked from sleep, and answered.

*Knock. Knock. Knock.*

And from below came a sound Ugo had never heard in all that time.

Breath was laughing.

«Who are you talking to?» asked Teaspoon, from the door.

«The one in the ceiling,» said Breath. «They'd gone to sleep.»

«All winter?»

«All winter. I knew it.»

Somebody who's asleep hasn't gone away, my love.

Sometimes somebody you love goes away for a while: to sleep, to work, far off. And for a while you knock, and nobody answers.

It doesn't mean they're not there any more. It only means it's winter.

And winters end. All of them.

And now close your eyes.

Above the meadow everything is quiet, under the roof somebody is breathing, and tomorrow there will be light again.

Goodnight. 🌙✨
