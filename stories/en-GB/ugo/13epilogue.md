# Epilogue

*Ugo's stories — to be read after all the others*

---

There. This one's finished too.

And I know what you're about to ask. We both know by now.

*But is it true?*

Let's do what we did the other times: I'll tell you what's true, piece by piece.

Bats are real, and they're not blind. They have eyes and they can see. But to get about in the dark they do just what Ugo does: they call out with a voice so high we can't hear it, and they listen to how it comes back. From the way it comes back they understand everything: where the tree is, where the wall is, where the mosquito is.

And they really do eat mosquitoes. Lots and lots of them, in a single night.

Fireflies, on the other hand, it seems they don't. People say that to bats fireflies taste bad, and that the light is partly there to say so: *not for you*. Not everybody is sure. But I like to think it's true.

Moths and some crickets are real too: they can hear bats' voices, and when they hear them they drop into the grass to get away. All except one, at least in this story.

And owls really do fly without making a sound. And some shrews really do make very high squeaks, to find their way in the dark. How much they talk, nobody has studied.

Bats live a long time. Much longer than you'd think: some reach more than thirty years. And in winter they sleep the long sleep, and their hearts really do slow down so much they seem almost to stop.

And they're protected. In lots of places, here too, you're not allowed to block up the hole a bat lives in. It's the law.

But nobody blocked up the hole under the tiles for a different reason.

I'd asked them not to.

Do you remember who said «Leave the hole. There's somebody there»? Breath, Long Stride. That was me. I don't know what he really called me. But I think he knew me by my breathing.

And Ugo is the name I gave him. Like Nina, like Tilde. His own name, his real one, is made of a voice I can't hear, and I'll never know it.

Heavy Step and Teaspoon, on the other hand, you know too. They still climb the stairs, slowly, and they still stir their sugar three times. Never four.

I don't know if Ugo is still there. Bats live a long time, but not for ever. But up there, under the tiles, there's somebody. I know because I count them in the evenings.

Tomorrow, if you like, we'll do it together.

We'll go into the garden a little before it gets dark, while the sky is still light. We'll stand under the roof, on the side with the hole with the hat, and look up without making a sound. And when the first one comes out, you count it. Then the second. Then the third.

You won't hear them calling. But they are calling, I promise. And they're answering each other.

And one more thing.

If tonight, in the dark, you hear scratching in the ceiling, don't be frightened.

Knock three times. Softly.

I don't know if they'll answer you.

They used to answer me.

*«Will you tell it again tomorrow?»*

Yes, my love.

Tomorrow and every evening.

Goodnight. 🌙✨
