# The meadow holds its breath

*A bedtime story — Book Eight, following "The lamp goes out"*

---

Ever since the lamp had gone out, Ugo's evenings always began the same way.

He came out of the hole under the tiles, *scritch, scritch-scritch, tick*, and at that very moment, down below, Breath's window went dark. And away at the bottom of the hill the meadow lit up all at once.

Ugo had grown fond of it. It almost felt as if he were the one lighting it, just by coming out.

Then, one evening, the window went dark, the meadow lit up, and something new happened.

The door opened.

Ugo knew that door. He heard it in the daytime, in his sleep, when the ones down there came and went. But at night it had never opened.

And out of the door came footsteps. Not Heavy Step, not Teaspoon. Short, uncertain steps that stopped every so often and then set off again.

And with the steps, a breath. Short and quick, like on the very first evenings.

Breath was going down the hill. Alone. In the dark.

Ugo followed from above, wide and silent, the way you follow somebody who mustn't know they're being followed.

Breath went down slowly. The tall grass came up to their tummy. At one point a white light came on, hard and straight, sweeping to and fro: a torch. It didn't bother Ugo; he looked with his voice.

But in the meadow, somebody else took fright.

Because to anyone who lives in the grass, whoever comes down the hill isn't small. They're a giant. The grass bends at every step, the ground shakes. And the fireflies, who a moment before had been a town full of lights, did something Ugo had never seen them do.

They went out. All together. Two hundred lanterns gone in a second.

Breath reached the middle of the meadow and stopped.

The torch searched everywhere: in the grass, in the hedge, on the stones in the brook. It found nothing. Then it went out too, and Ugo heard a rustle: Breath had sat down in the wet grass.

And the breathing became the shortest it had ever been.

Ugo knew it too well to be wrong. Breath was afraid. They had come all the way down here, in the dark, alone, to see the lights. And the lights had vanished. And now all around there was only black, and the black seemed empty.

But Ugo, from above, called.

And his voice came back full.

The dark wasn't empty at all. Under every clover leaf there was a firefly with its lantern closed, small and still, holding its breath. Two hundred fireflies holding their breath. Under the root of the oak there was an old cricket with his antennae raised, listening. There was a snail halfway across a stone. There was the brook, carrying on as always. And somewhere in the grass, for once in his life, even Pino was quiet.

The whole meadow was there, all around Breath. The whole meadow was keeping still, and waiting.

Ugo would have liked to tell them. *You're not alone. They're all here. They're very close.*

But there was no ceiling to knock on. And his voice, he knew, wouldn't be heard.

So he did the only thing he could. He stayed. He circled over the meadow, wide and slow, for as long as it took. The way you stay close to somebody even when they don't know you're there.

A very long time passed. Breath didn't move. And little by little, Ugo heard the breathing change: less short. Less quick. Like somebody who, instead of being afraid of the dark, begins to listen to it.

And then, in the dark, a single little light came on.

Then another, pale green, two steps further on. Then three. Then ten. Then the whole meadow, a piece at a time, the way a town lights up when evening comes.

And Breath's breathing stopped altogether. And then it started again, long, very long, as if Breath had been waiting their whole life to breathe like that.

From deep in the grass a song rose up, *chirr*, crooked and thin and beautiful. And Ugo, up above, understood that he wasn't needed any more that night. And he went off to have his supper.

But the night wasn't over.

Later, hunting on the far side of the meadow, where the grass is shorter, Ugo heard a voice. An old voice, slow and calm, talking to a firefly. The firefly was terribly excited, and was saying the meadow was lit up as never before, and that somebody had come down from the hill...

«I'll take your word for it,» said the old voice.

Ugo didn't know whose voice it was. He had never heard it outside the ground.

And soon afterwards, from the same spot, came a long, long sound. A crackling, like ice when it starts to split from a single point and then doesn't stop. Something, down there under the grass, was opening.

Ugo didn't go closer. Some things are done alone, or in twos.

At dawn, heading back towards the roof, tired and with a full tummy, Ugo passed something in the sky he had never heard before.

It was flying upwards. Badly, crookedly, the way somebody flies when they're doing it for the very first time. Its wings, to his echo, were still soft. And inside it, just barely, something hummed.

Ugo moved aside and let it go by.

Then he slipped into the hole under the tiles, *tick*, and hung himself from his beam.

Below him, in bed, Breath was back. And sleeping long.

That night, all over the meadow, everyone had had their own story. The one who came down the hill. The one who lit up first. The one who listened from under a root. The one who took somebody's word for it. The one who grew wings.

And only one, up above, had heard them all together.

Nobody knew. Nobody had asked him. But before he fell asleep Ugo went over them all, one by one, the way you count precious things.

When you keep still in the dark, my love, you're never as alone as you think.

The dark seems empty because you can't see anything. But it's full: of somebody breathing, of somebody waiting, of somebody holding their breath along with you.

Tonight, if you wake up and it's dark, remember that. There are more people around you than you can see.

And now close your eyes.

Above the meadow everything is quiet, under the roof somebody is breathing, and tomorrow there will be light again.

Goodnight. 🌙✨
