# The hole under the tiles

*A bedtime story — Book Eleven, following "The lights over there"*

---

The house at the top of the hill wasn't new any more.

Ugo had noticed from the sounds. The beams creaked more. Some of the tiles rattled in the wind. And Heavy Step, on the stairs, didn't make anything shake any more: they climbed one stair at a time now, with a pause in between. Teaspoon's *tink tink tink* still came at the same time every evening, but slower.

And Breath wasn't there any more.

That is, they were. But that wasn't their name any more. For a long while now, the step coming up the stairs had been a new one: long and calm, taking the stairs without hurry and without effort. Ugo, to himself, had started calling it Long Stride. But he knew perfectly well who it was. Some things you recognise even when they change their name.

One summer morning, while Ugo was asleep hanging from his beam, the roof opened.

Not gently. With a bang, then another, then the sound of tiles being lifted off one by one and put down somewhere, *clack, clack, clack*. And with the tiles gone, in came the light.

Ugo woke with a start.

Light, for a bat, means it's time to sleep. But light like this, indoors, in the middle of the day, he had never seen. It came in in stripes through the open roof, white and hot, and with the light came new voices, big ones, and heavy steps over his head. Steps walking *on the roof*.

It was the builders. The ones down there had decided to redo the roof.

Ugo dragged himself into the darkest corner he could find, at the far end of the beam, where the tiles were still in place. He wrapped himself tight in his wings. And he waited, his heart beating as hard as it hadn't beaten for many winters.

The builders worked all day. They took off old tiles and put on new ones. They whistled. And little by little they came closer to Ugo's corner.

Then one of them stopped.

«There's a hole here,» he said. «Under the tiles, on the edge. We'll fill it in.»

Ugo felt it with his whole body. His hole. The one he had come out of every evening all his life, *scritch, scritch-scritch, tick*. The only door he had.

It wasn't unkindness, and Ugo knew it. To somebody who doesn't know, a roof with a hole in it is a broken roof. And a broken roof gets mended.

«Pass me the mortar,» said the builder.

And from below, from the garden, came a voice.

«Wait.»

Ugo knew it. He had heard it change over all those years, through the ceiling: first a little voice, then a voice, then this one. It was Long Stride.

«Leave the hole,» they said. «There's somebody there.»

On the roof there was a silence.

«Somebody who?» asked the builder.

«I don't know,» said Long Stride. «I've never seen them. But they're there.»

The builder laughed a little, not unkindly. «It'll be the wind.»

«It's not the wind,» said Long Stride. «The wind doesn't answer when you knock.»

And another voice, from below, older and slower, said: «Leave it.» It was Teaspoon. «If they say there's somebody there, there's somebody there. They said so back when they slept up there too, and nobody ever believed them.»

Ugo, in his corner, didn't move.

The builders talked among themselves. Then they did something Ugo hadn't expected. They didn't fill in the hole. They put a little wooden board over it, sticking out a bit, like a tiny roof: so that the rain wouldn't get in, but so that somebody could still get out underneath.

Then they put the tiles back all round it, and went away.

That evening Ugo waited until it was completely dark. Then he dragged himself towards the hole, slowly, his heart still a little tight.

*Scritch. Scritch-scritch.*

The hole was there. The same shape as always. Only now it had a hat.

*Tick.*

He went out.

And down below, in the garden, there was somebody. Standing up, perfectly still, nose in the air, looking at the roof.

Long Stride was waiting. Waiting to see who came out.

Ugo felt them with his echo: the long shape, arms by their sides, the breathing. And the breathing was long and calm, just as it had been that night so many years before, when the lights in the meadow came back on.

For the first time in his life, one of the ones down there was looking at him.

And Long Stride saw him. A small dark thing slipping off the roof, opening its wings and wheeling in the sky.

«Ah,» they said softly. «So it was you.»

They weren't afraid. Ugo could hear it perfectly: not even a little. It was the voice of somebody who has finally found something they'd been looking for for a long time, and finds it's smaller than they'd imagined, and that that's fine.

So Ugo did something he had never done before.

He came down. Low, very low, almost to the grass. And he flew over Long Stride's head, so close that the air from his wings ruffled their hair.

Just once. Then he climbed again, and went off towards the brook, to have his supper.

And behind him, in the garden, he heard Long Stride laugh. Like that time, in spring, when the ceiling had answered after a whole winter.

Somebody who was afraid of you, my love, sometimes becomes the very one who stands up for you.

It happens, when you get to know each other a little. Something that frightened you, once you know it, becomes something you look after. And a noise in the ceiling, if you answer it for enough evenings, becomes somebody you'll say *wait* for.

Keep that in mind, the next time something frightens you. Perhaps, one day, you'll be the one standing up for it.

And now close your eyes.

Above the meadow everything is quiet, under the roof somebody is breathing, and tomorrow there will be light again.

Goodnight. 🌙✨
