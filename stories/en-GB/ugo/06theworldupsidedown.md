# The world upside down

*A bedtime story — Book Six, following "The night Pino was quiet"*

---

Pino had a question.

Pino always had a question, but this one had been going round in his head for days. And one evening, lying on his back in the middle of the grass, he asked it.

«Ugo?»

Silence. On purpose.

«Yes?»

«Is it true you sleep upside down?»

«Yes.»

«All day?»

«All day.»

«And nothing falls out? Doesn't all your blood go to your feet? I mean, to your head? Doesn't it feel like the sky's on the ground? And your dreams, are they upside down too? And if you need to sneeze, where does the sneeze go?»

Then he remembered the rule, and was quiet.

«I don't know,» said Ugo. «I've never thought about it. I'm comfortable like that.»

Pino wasn't convinced. To Pino, *comfortable* meant one thing only: a dry hole, a little heap of leaves, and your tummy on the ground. How can anybody be comfortable hanging by their feet?

So he decided to try.

The first night he climbed up a blade of grass. The blade of grass bent over, and Pino found himself on the ground, on his back, with grass in his mouth.

«Too soft,» he said.

The second night he tried a twig in the hedge. He gripped it with his back legs, let himself go little by little... and there he stayed, upside down, swinging like a pear.

«There!» he shouted. «There! I've done it! Ugo, look, I've done it!»

Then his feet slipped, and he fell into the leaves.

«I don't understand,» he said, from the leaves. «I don't understand how you do it. And most of all I don't understand why.»

And he was quiet. Because this time he really wanted the answer.

So Ugo explained. It took a while, because every so often Pino forgot the rule and they had to wait for him to finish.

He explained that a bat can't run. His legs are small and crooked, made for holding on, not for walking. A bird, to take off, jumps. A firefly lets go of a blade of grass. But a bat on the ground, to fly, would need a run-up: and since he can't run, it's a mess.

Hanging upside down, though, he doesn't have to do a thing. He opens his wings, lets go, and he's already flying. Like somebody who wakes up and finds they're already dressed.

«And besides,» said Ugo, «up there under the roof nobody comes looking for me. Anyone who wanted to eat me would have to walk on the ceiling.»

Pino thought about it. He imagined himself walking on the ceiling, and it made him laugh.

«So you sleep upside down because that way you're already ready to go.»

«Yes.»

«And you're safe.»

«Yes.»

«And you're comfortable.»

«Yes. For me it's the most comfortable way there is.»

«But not for me.»

«Not for you.»

Pino was quiet for a very long moment, for him.

«Then both ways are fine,» he said.

Then, because he was Pino, he wanted to try one last time anyway.

He climbed up a low root, very very low, so that if he fell he wouldn't fall far. He hung by his back legs. He let himself go.

And the world turned over.

The grass hung down from above like a fringe. The sky was down below him: a great black pool full of stars, so deep it looked as if you could fall right into it. And the house at the top of the hill was hanging from the meadow by its roof, just like Ugo from his beam.

«Ugo,» said Pino, in a small voice. «It's beautiful.»

«I know.»

«My head's spinning.»

«I know that too. At first.»

«Did yours spin too?»

Ugo thought about it. He couldn't remember. Perhaps it had, a long time ago, the very first time, in the middle of the others.

«Perhaps,» he said.

Pino stayed hanging a little longer, looking at the sky underneath. Then he climbed down, settled into his leaves tummy-down, with his tummy on the ground, just how he liked it, and gave a happy sigh.

«The world upside down is beautiful,» he said. «But I sleep the right way up.»

«And I sleep the wrong way up,» said Ugo.

And they both went to sleep: Pino that very night, and Ugo the next morning, when the light came. Because that's upside down too, for a bat: light means it's time for bed.

Everybody has their own way of being comfortable, my love.

Some people sleep on their tummy and some on their back, some with the light on and some in the dark, some curled up like a hedgehog and some spread out like a star.

Your way might look strange to somebody. That doesn't mean it's wrong: it only means that somebody, to be comfortable, does it differently.

Tonight, find yours, and settle into it.

And now close your eyes.

Above the meadow everything is quiet, under the roof somebody is breathing, and tomorrow there will be light again.

Goodnight. 🌙✨
