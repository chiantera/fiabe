# The answer

*A bedtime story — Book Twelve, following "The hole under the tiles"*

---

That autumn, under the roof, somebody new began to breathe.

Ugo noticed one October evening, as he was getting ready to go out. From the room beneath his beam, the one with the low, slanting ceiling, where nobody had slept for a long time, came a breath. Small. Short and quick. Like somebody who doesn't know the dark yet.

Ugo knew that breath. Not that one: one just like it. He had heard it before, many years ago.

And that evening, from the bed, he heard another voice too, long and calm. Long Stride's, sitting on the edge of the bed.

«If you hear scratching in the ceiling,» they were saying, «don't be frightened. Knock three times.»

Then silence. Then, softly, from below:

*Knock. Knock. Knock.*

Ugo stretched out his claw, a little more crooked than it used to be, and answered.

*Knock. Knock. Knock.*

And below, after a moment, the new breath grew a little longer.

Then November came, and with November the long sleep.

Ugo was a very old bat by now. The long sleep came more easily than it used to, and left more slowly. He hung from his beam, wrapped himself in his wings, and the last thing he heard was three knocks, from below, and his own heart slowing down.

He slept all winter.

In March, one evening, he opened an eye.

His heart started again, one turn at a time. His wings were stiff, his legs were numb, and he was as hungry as ever. But something was different, and it took Ugo a while to work out what.

It was warm.

Not the warmth of spring: that hadn't come yet. A small, close warmth, coming from the dark beside him. Like when you sleep squeezed up tight, lots of you together, to keep warm.

Ugo didn't move.

It couldn't be. Under that roof there had only ever been him. All his life, every evening, he had called, and the roof had answered with his own voice, close and kind, like somebody answering you from the next room.

It was his way of knowing where he was. A bat always calls.

So he did what he did every evening. Before going out, before knowing, before hoping anything at all.

Ugo called.

And for the first time in many, many years, what came back was not his own voice.

It was voices. Small, very high, new. One, then three, then ten, from the dark beside him, all along the beam. Voices calling each other and answering, the way they used to, in the hollow tree across the brook. The loveliest sound in the world.

It wasn't his colony. Ugo knew at once: he knew all those voices from long ago, and these were different. Younger. From who knows where.

«Who are you?» asked one, very close.

«Ugo,» said Ugo. «I live here.»

«Since when?»

Ugo thought about it.

«Since always,» he said.

They had arrived at the end of the winter, while he was asleep. They were looking for somewhere dark, dry and warm, and they had found a hole under the tiles. A hole with a little board over it, like a hat.

«You could tell it was a door,» said the voice. «Somebody had left it open on purpose.»

«Yes,» said Ugo. «Somebody had.»

That evening they went out together.

One after another, out of the hole with the hat, *scritch, scritch-scritch, tick*, *tick*, *tick*. And the sky over the meadow filled with voices nobody could hear: not the fireflies, not the crickets, not people.

But they could. They could all hear each other.

And down at the bottom of the hill the meadow lit up, as it did every evening. And under the roof, in the room with the slanting ceiling, a new breath grew long.

Keep calling, my love. Even when only your own voice comes back.

Calling is never wasted. It tells you where you are. It keeps your heart awake. And it gets you ready for that evening, which may come late, maybe very late, when somebody answers.

Because somebody, sooner or later, answers.

And now close your eyes.

Above the meadow everything is quiet, under the roof somebody is breathing, and tomorrow there will be light again.

Goodnight. 🌙✨
