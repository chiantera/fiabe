# The lights over there

*A bedtime story — Book Ten, following "Somebody turned"*

---

Another summer came, and then another September.

The nights grew longer. The mosquitoes over the brook began to thin out, and Ugo, who was an old bat by now, felt in his wings that gentle tiredness that means: soon, the long sleep.

One of those evenings he came out of the hole under the tiles, *tick*, and found something strange.

The meadow was lit up, as always. But it wasn't going out.

Usually, after a while, the fireflies settled down, one here and one there, and the town grew smaller. Not that night. Two hundred lights, all together, from one side of the meadow to the other, steady and strong. And the crooked song, which wouldn't stop.

Down below, Breath's window went dark as it did every evening. But behind the dark glass Ugo heard somebody who wasn't sleeping. They were standing up, with their hands resting on the sill, looking out. Their breathing was long and slow, like somebody looking at something beautiful who doesn't yet know why it's beautiful.

Ugo went down towards the meadow.

And he saw something nobody at the window could see.

At the hedge, at the far end of the meadow, there were two little lights side by side, not moving: one golden and one pale green. They stayed there for a moment. Then the golden one crossed the hedge, alone. And the pale green one turned back, towards the meadow, and began to sing louder than all the rest.

Beyond the hedge, Ugo knew, the grass came to an end. There began a pale, dry land, with no flowers, no water, no mosquitoes. He flew over it now and then, in a hurry, because there was nothing to eat. And fireflies never went there.

But that little light was going.

It flew low and slow, not strong, not fast, like somebody with old wings. And it went straight, like somebody who knows where they're going even though they don't know the way.

Ugo followed it.

He kept wide, very high, as quiet as a bat can be. He didn't want to frighten it. He didn't even want to help it: he wouldn't have known how. He only wanted to see where it went.

He followed it over the pale land for a long way. Behind, the meadow grew small, and the song thinner. Ahead, the little light went on, always the same.

At a certain point it was so small that Ugo couldn't tell it apart from the low stars any more.

And there he stopped. He circled once, twice. And he turned back.

Because some journeys belong to one alone. And because, although nobody had ever told him, he knew: that light meant *not for you*. Not in the old way. In a new one.

That night the meadow stayed lit until morning. And Ugo, heading back to the roof as the sky grew light, heard that behind the glass there was still somebody, standing up, looking out.

Then many summers went by.

Ugo didn't count them. Bats live a long time, much longer than people think, and after a while they stop counting. There came the summer when Breath, down below, began to walk with a different step, a longer one. There came the summer when Pino managed to stay quiet for ten whole seconds in a row, and told the whole meadow about it for a week.

And the song, in the meadow, carried on. Every evening, without missing one.

Then one autumn night, a cold, clear night, Ugo asked himself something he had never asked before.

*How far does it go?*

The song. The crooked one the meadow sang every evening. How far away could it be heard?

So he flew to the hedge. And he crossed it.

He flew over the pale land, the way he had that night so many years before. The meadow behind him grew small, then tiny, then a speck of lights. And the song grew thin, thinner and thinner, until it was a thread. A thread you could only just hear, even with a bat's ears.

Ugo went on a little further. The thread was about to run out.

And right there, where the thread was about to run out, he saw a golden dot.

It was tiny. And it was breathing: it lit up and went out, slowly, slowly, the way a living thing does when it's in no hurry at all.

Ugo stopped in mid-air.

And further on, perhaps, there was another. Perhaps. It was too far away to be sure.

He could have called. His voice would have gone ahead into the dark, bumped against that small thing, and come back to tell him what it was: a firefly, or a shiny stone, or only a low star, the kind that looks as if it's resting on the ground.

He didn't call.

He stayed there a moment, watching the dot breathing. Then he made a wide turn, and went home, following the thread of the song until it was a whole song again.

He didn't tell anyone.

Nobody would have heard him anyway. But even if they had, Ugo wouldn't have told them. Some things, when you say them, get smaller.

He hung from his beam, wrapped in his wings. And before he slept he thought that the song reached a very long way. Further than anyone in the meadow knew.

And that perhaps, over there, somebody could hear it.

Sometimes the loveliest news is known only by somebody who can't tell it, my love.

That doesn't mean it isn't true. It only means that some lovely things happen far away, in the dark, where hardly anyone sees them.

And that if you sing anyway, every evening, perhaps they reach all the way there.

And now close your eyes.

Above the meadow everything is quiet, under the roof somebody is breathing, and tomorrow there will be light again.

Goodnight. 🌙✨
