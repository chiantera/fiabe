# Prologue

*Ugo's stories — to be read before all the others*

---

I told you the firefly's story because I saw it with my own eyes.

I told you the mole's story on trust, because somebody had told it to me.

This one is different. This one I heard.

I heard it for years, every evening, through the ceiling. And I didn't understand it. I only understood it when I was grown up, a little piece at a time, and some pieces I've only understood now, while I was getting it ready for you.

There's something I have to tell you first.

When I was your age I slept in a room with a low, slanting ceiling, right under the roof. If you stood up on the bed, you could touch it with your hand.

And every evening, when I was put to bed, a noise came from the ceiling.

A small scratching. *Scritch. Scritch-scritch.* And then a *tick*.

I was too frightened to sleep. I said so, and they always gave me the same answer: that there was nothing there. That it was the wind. That I should go to sleep.

It wasn't the wind.

One evening I did something I had never done before: standing on the bed, trembling like a leaf, I knocked. Three times.

And the ceiling answered me.

I won't tell you the rest, because it's inside the story, and you'll hear the story one evening at a time. I'll only tell you that from that evening on, the noise in the ceiling didn't frighten me any more. Quite the opposite.

For years I knocked, and for years somebody answered. And I didn't know who it was. I only knew they were there.

What went on up there, above the ceiling and outside, in the dark, over the meadow, I couldn't see. Some things I found out later. Others I imagined, by listening carefully, because when you listen to a noise for enough years you end up knowing what the one who makes it is like.

Now that room with the slanting ceiling is yours.

And when you're in bed, and you turn out the light, above you, under the tiles, there's still somebody there.

Don't be frightened. I'm telling you first, so you know: whoever is up there has never hurt anybody. They only have a voice that nobody can hear.

This is their story.

It begins like this:

*Above the meadow at the foot of the hill, at night, there flew somebody nobody had ever heard.*
