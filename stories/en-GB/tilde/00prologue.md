# Prologue

*Tilde's stories — to be read before all the others*

---

I told you the firefly's stories because I saw them with my own eyes. I was there at the window that night, and I turned off the lamp myself, and I was the first one to see the meadow light up.

This one I'll tell you differently.

This one I didn't see. Somebody told it to me, many years later, one evening when I was already grown up — and I believed them, on their word, the way you always do, in the end, with the stories that really matter. You'll see, when we get there, that this is exactly what happens inside the story too.

But first there's something I have to tell you, and this time I've never told it before.

Do you remember the house at the top of the hill? The one with the yellow window, the one I watched the meadow light up gold from? It was a good house. The people who lived in it cared about things, and they wouldn't have hurt anybody on purpose, or even by accident, if only they'd known how.

But when they built it, they also dug a well.

And nobody up there ever wondered where the water goes when you move it to dig a well. That water ended up right under the meadow, in a place where somebody had lived their whole life, and it changed course without asking anybody's permission.

In the stories I've already told you, that house was the light in the window: the wonder, the start of a friendship. In these ones it's something else. It's the same house, with the same good people inside it. Only seen from underneath, that house did real harm — and nobody up top knew for a very long time, and by the time they found out, it was too late to put it fully right.

I wanted to tell you this before, not after, because it's an important thing to know from early on: good people can do real harm. Not because they're bad. Because they don't know the meadow has an underneath too.

Under that meadow, in those same years, through one very long night beneath the grass, somebody else was living.

She had no wings, and she made no light, and nobody up above knew her name.

But she held a whole world together, one corridor at a time, and I want to tell you this story exactly the way it was told to me: on somebody's word.

It starts like this:

*In the meadow at the foot of the hill, under the grass, there lived a mole called Tilde.*
