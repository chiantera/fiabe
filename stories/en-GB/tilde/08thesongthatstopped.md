# The song that stopped

*A bedtime story — Book Eight, following "The room that isn't there any more"*

---

That summer, one evening, the ceiling didn't tremble.

Tilde noticed a little late, because she had dozed off: these days it happened to her, dozing off in the evening before it was even properly dark, and waking up with no idea how much time had passed.

She waited a while, because some evenings Rocco started late.

Then she waited some more.

Then she went up under the big root and knocked three times: *I'm here.*

Nobody answered.

Tilde stood there with her paw raised, in a silence that had never been so silent, because it wasn't just any silence: it was the silence of a thing that had always been there.

She knocked again. Nothing.

So she dug straight up, which you never do — it ruins the ceiling, and a ruined ceiling comes down on your head sooner or later — and came out among the roots, off to one side, where the earth is dry.

Rocco was there.

He was alive, and he was very old, and the moment he saw Tilde he turned his head away.

«Hello,» said Tilde.

Rocco moved his antennae and said nothing.

Tilde waited. She was good at waiting: underground you do a great deal of it.

And after a while Rocco spoke, and the voice that came out was a thread.

«It won't come any more,» he said. «I've been trying all evening.»

Tilde didn't say *try again*, and didn't say *never mind*, and didn't say *of course it will come*.

She sat down.

«I can still feel you,» she said, after a bit.

«I'm not doing anything.»

«I can feel you breathing. It comes through the root.»

Rocco didn't answer, but he turned his head back a little.

They stayed there until late, saying almost nothing.

When Tilde went back down, she didn't go to bed.

She went into the corridor under the oak, put herself alongside the big root, and started thinking like a mole.

*Cold air pulls from here.* That was the north side: winter would come from there.

*It's damp here.* There was still water underneath, not much, but there.

*Here, though, it isn't.* Under the curve of the root, on the shaded side, there was a pocket of pale dry earth the water had never touched in all those years, because the root made a roof for it.

Tilde started digging.

It took her three nights, and she did all three in secret.

She widened the pocket by just as much as a cricket needs. She took out the little stones one by one. She smoothed the walls with her nose, the way you do for the good rooms. She left the opening small and turned to the south, so the winter wind would pass beside it instead of into it. And at the back, where the ground was softest, she pushed in a handful of dry grass she had been keeping.

Then she closed it all up behind her and went away without telling anybody.

Rocco moved in a week later.

He didn't ask who had done it. Perhaps he knew and didn't want to make a business of it, or perhaps he simply thought the place had always been there and he hadn't noticed before: that happens.

Everybody called it «the little corner of dry earth under the root of the oak», and nobody in the meadow ever wondered why the earth should be dry just there.

The song never came back.

But for all the evenings that were left, when it went dark, Tilde would go up into the corridor under the root and knock three times on the ceiling.

And from the ceiling, very faintly, three knocks would come back.

When you can't fix the thing, fix another one, my love.

Nearly always the right one can't be fixed: you can't give anybody their voice back, you can't bring back a thing that has gone.

But nearly always, somewhere near it, there's another one that can be done. Smaller, and crooked, and nothing to do with it.

Do that one. It counts just the same, and sometimes it counts more.

And now close your eyes.

Under the meadow almost everything is quiet, almost all the roads are where they should be, and tomorrow there will be light again.

Goodnight. 🌙✨
