# The front door

*A bedtime story — Book Twelve, following "Seven leaves"*

---

The new house was almost finished.

Tilde had joined her own stretch to the ancient city's, sorted out the larder, reopened two old exits, and closed off a branch that wasn't any use to anyone any more. Only one thing was left, the thing that matters most in a house and that nobody ever thinks about until it isn't there.

The door was missing.

Or rather: she hadn't decided where the entrance would go — the exact spot in the grass where the main corridor would break through into the open air.

Tilde thought about it for two whole evenings, which for her was a very long time, because usually she decided things simply by digging and nothing else.

Then she said to Pino: «I'm putting it by the big oak root.»

«Why there?»

Tilde didn't answer straight away.

«Because that's where Rocco's room was», she said, finally. «And because I still know exactly where it is, even with my eyes shut.»

Pino didn't ask anything else. He too knew how to count the knocks that no longer came from the ceiling.

Tilde dug the last stretch calmly, the way you always do the last stretch. She came out into the open right under the shade of the oak, one step from where, for years, she'd knocked three times on a ceiling that used to answer.

And the house was finished.

That evening Pino turned up all cheerful, as usual, ready for the job he always did.

«I'm here!» he said. «Shall I walk you home?»

Because that was how it had always gone: Tilde would go out, take a few steps, turn round, find three identical tufts of grass in front of her, ask *was it this one?*, get told no, and then somebody would walk her home.

«No need», said Tilde.

Pino's mouth fell open.

«What do you mean, no need?»

«No need, Pino. I'm going on my own.»

And she said it with a calm that Pino didn't like one bit, because he wasn't used to that calm from her, and it felt as though something was being taken away from him.

Tilde took a few steps into the grass. She turned round.

And she found it.

First try. For the first time in her whole life.

There wasn't even a moment's doubt, not even a *was it this one?*: there was the entrance, exactly where she'd put it, exactly under the shade of the oak, and Tilde walked straight to it as if she'd done it a thousand times before, which, in a way, she had — only all those other thousand times it had been somebody else's house leading her there.

«But how did you do it?» asked Pino, in the small voice of somebody who has just lost a job.

Tilde thought about it, the way she'd done once before, a great many years earlier, when they'd asked her the same thing about the hedgehog in the crack.

«I never got lost because I know the grass», she said. «I got lost because I never chose the entrance myself. This one I chose. I put it where I know it always is, because it's the one place I never forget.»

Pino went quiet a moment, which for him was very rare indeed.

«And me?» he asked, after a while. «If you don't get lost any more, what am I for?»

Tilde looked at him.

«You're still for the same thing», she said. «Only now, when you come, it's because you want to. Not because I need somebody to find me.»

Pino wasn't entirely convinced, but decided he was fine with it anyway, and stayed there chatting for a while before going off to find something to eat, as usual.

Tilde stayed outside a little longer, near the new entrance, under the oak that never speaks and that, meanwhile, an inch a year, kept moving its roots exactly where it had always meant to move them.

She didn't knock the three knocks. There was nothing left above to knock for.

But she stayed there a moment anyway, the way you stay near a place that matters, even when nobody answers any more.

Then she went in, and for the first time in her life she didn't need to tell anybody: she already knew she was home.

Home isn't where you were put, my love.

It's the one you built yourself, piece by piece, maybe getting a curve wrong the first time and fixing it the second. It's the one whose entrance you chose, and that's why you always find it again, even when everything around you looks like three identical tufts of grass.

And sometimes you put the front door exactly there, right by the memory of somebody who isn't there any more: not to stay sad, but because it's still the one spot you never forget.

And now close your eyes.

Under the meadow everything is quiet, the roads are where they should be, and tomorrow there will be light again.

Goodnight. 🌙✨
