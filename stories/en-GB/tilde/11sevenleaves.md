# Seven leaves

*A bedtime story — Book Eleven, following "The city that came before"*

---

One summer night, in the seventh year, Seven said: «Tilde.»

And there was something in the voice that had never been there before.

«I'm ready.»

Tilde didn't ask ready for what. She'd known for seven years.

«Now?»

«Now.»

Seven began moving upwards, slowly, with a movement she'd never made before: until then she had only stayed; now she was going.

Tilde followed her. But first she settled her spectacles properly on her nose — to do things right, even though up there she'd need them no more than she did down below, which is to say not at all.

They climbed through corridors Tilde knew by heart, and the further up they went, the more the air changed — less damp, then cold, then with something in it that Tilde didn't care for at all and which is called wind.

Tilde never went up top unless she was made to. And now she was being made to.

They reached the last stretch, the one that broke through into the open, and Seven stopped.

«I can't dig any more on my own», she said. «My legs aren't good for it now. I need somebody else to do the last bit.»

Tilde dug the last bit.

And the darkness below turned into a different kind of darkness: a darkness with air moving inside it, and sounds coming from far away instead of close by.

Outside.

Tilde didn't put her nose to it straight away.

«What's it like?» she asked.

«I don't know yet», said Seven. «Nearly there.»

They came out together, Seven first and Tilde right behind, and Tilde found herself, for the first time in seven years, standing in the meadow grass at night with nothing over her head but nothing at all.

She could hardly see anything. Moles aren't made for that, and she was perfectly happy about it.

But she could hear.

And what she heard was that the meadow, that night, was not as quiet as other nights. There was a hum, far away and close at the same time, as if the air itself were trembling a little along its whole length.

A firefly went past, and Tilde felt her go by from the change in the air.

«Hello», said the firefly. «You're the mole. You're never out here.»

«I never come up», said Tilde. «Tonight I'm seeing off a friend.»

«It's a special night, you know. The meadow's lit up all over. I've never seen anything like it. It's as if somebody's lit up a whole city, all at once, from one side to the other — there's a child come down from the hill and—»

«I'll take your word for it», said Tilde.

It wasn't rudeness. It was the truest thing she could say: she couldn't see any of it, and she couldn't have cared less about that, and yet she believed every word, because the firefly had no reason to lie to her.

The firefly went on, still full of wonder, towards her part of the lit-up meadow.

Tilde stayed where she was, beside Seven.

And that was when Seven began.

First a small crackle, like ice starting to split from a single point. Then the crackle stretched out, slow, very long, and it went on — Tilde couldn't have said how long, because right then time started doing something strange.

«Does it hurt?» Tilde asked, at one point.

«No», said Seven, in a voice different from before, smaller and bigger at once. «But it's long.»

«Long things are», said Tilde. «I say that all the time.»

The crackling went on. Tilde stayed put, seeing nothing, hearing everything: the sound of the old shell opening, and under that sound another sound, very small, of something new drying in the air for the first time.

Then, at some point, there was silence.

«Seven?»

«I'm here», said a voice. And it was Seven's voice, but it wasn't her old voice any more: it was a voice with something humming inside it, a small barely-there thrum, the first note of something that would grow much louder.

«How are you?»

«I have wings», said Seven. «Tilde. I have wings.»

She didn't say it as a question. She said it the way somebody says a true thing for the first time, with a voice that still doesn't quite believe it.

They stayed still a moment, Tilde on the ground and Seven beside her, wings drying slowly in the dark.

«I'm going up now», said Seven.

«I know.»

«You won't be able to come.»

«I know.»

«I don't know when I'll be back. Maybe never. Cicadas don't always come back to where they were born.»

Tilde didn't say *you'll come back*, and she didn't say *that's all right*, because she didn't know if either one was true.

She said: «Seven years is a very long time to know somebody, sitting still beside them. Go on.»

Seven stayed a moment longer, like somebody who can't quite find the strength to be the one who moves first.

Then Tilde felt the air change beside her — a movement unlike any she knew, not a crawl and not a walk: a rising.

And then she couldn't hear anything at all, and she understood that Seven had gone somewhere Tilde had no way of following, not even with her ears.

She stayed there a while longer, in the middle of the grass, under a sky she couldn't see, in a meadow that was, she'd been told, lit up all over that night.

Then she went back down.

She passed the empty cell — small, round, still holding the shape of seven years in the earth — and stopped a moment on the threshold.

She didn't close it up straight away.

She left it as it was for a while, the way you leave a door open when you still hope, just a little, that somebody might come back and knock.

Sometimes you see somebody as far as a certain point, my love, and then that point is theirs, not yours.

A first day of school. An airport. A door closing, and on the other side a whole piece of a life you'll never see, that you'll never know how it went.

It's a frightening thing, letting somebody go somewhere you can't follow.

But it's also the biggest gift you can give them: standing perfectly still right up to the edge, and not one step further, because that next step isn't yours to take.

Tilde has never seen the sky. She believed it anyway, from somebody who described it to her.

And now close your eyes.

Under the meadow almost everything is quiet, almost all the roads are where they should be, and tomorrow there will be light again.

Goodnight. 🌙✨
