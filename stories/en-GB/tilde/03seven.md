# Seven

*A bedtime story — Book Three, following "The roads under the meadow"*

---

Under the meadow, in a cell about this big, attached to a thin root, there was a creature doing nothing.

She wasn't asleep. She wasn't ill. She wasn't sad.

She was simply doing nothing, and she had been doing it for seven years.

Her name was Seven, and she was a cicada nymph.

Tilde had always known her. She had found her when she was the size of a grain of rice, the year she redid the east corridor, and ever since she looked in now and then to see how things were going.

«How's it going?»

«Fine,» Seven would say.

And that was that.

Tilde always sat down for a moment before leaving, because getting up quickly, these days, cost her a small «ouch» in her back legs.

One day Tilde took Pino down, who the moment he heard about the cell wanted to see it at once, and who, faced with Seven, stayed quiet for nearly two whole seconds.

Then he said: «What do you do?»

«I stay here,» said Seven.

«And then?»

«And then I stay here.»

Pino looked at Tilde, looked at the cell, looked at Seven again.

«For how long?»

«Seven years.»

Pino made a noise as though something had dropped inside his stomach.

«Seven *years*? What have you done in seven years?»

«This,» said Seven.

«But don't you get bored?»

And here Seven moved for the first time, only slightly, the way a person turns over in bed.

«No,» she said. «Because I'm not just sitting still. I'm becoming.»

«Becoming what?»

«I don't know yet. Something with wings.»

Pino, who in seven years would be born, grow up, grow old and die four times over, simply could not take it in.

«But it's wasted time!»

«Touch my back,» said Seven.

Pino put out a paw and touched.

«What's this hard stuff?»

«That wasn't there last year,» said Seven.

On the way out, Pino was unusually silent.

«It doesn't add up,» he said to Tilde. «Somebody sits still for seven years and something happens anyway?»

«Look around you,» said Tilde.

«I can't see anything, it's dark.»

«Exactly. Touch.»

Pino touched the corridor wall.

«That's a root,» said Tilde. «When I dug this stretch, that root wasn't there. Now it's as thick as my arm. I've never seen it move. Nobody has ever seen it move.»

They went on in the dark.

«The oak up there is fifty times your height,» said Tilde. «It came out of an acorn. Nobody ever watched it grow, not for a single afternoon. And yet there it is.»

Pino considered this, which cost him considerable physical effort.

«So the long things can't be seen while they're happening.»

«There you are,» said Tilde. «Good.»

That night Pino couldn't sleep, which for him meant staying awake twenty-two minutes together.

And every so often, in the years that followed, he would go back down to the cell on his own and sit outside it and ask: «How's it going?»

And Seven would say: «Fine.»

And he would stay a moment, and then dash off to eat.

There are things that take an enormously long time, my love.

Learning to read. Getting tall. Stopping being frightened of something.

And because they take so long, while they're happening it looks as though nothing is happening at all, and you want to say *I can't do it* or *this is wasted time*.

It isn't wasted time. It's just long.

Tonight as well, while you're asleep and doing absolutely nothing, you are growing.

And now close your eyes.

Under the meadow everything is quiet, the roads are where they should be, and tomorrow there will be light again.

Goodnight. 🌙✨
