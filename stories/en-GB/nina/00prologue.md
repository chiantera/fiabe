# Prologue

*Nina's stories — to be read before all the others*

---

Turn the big light off. Leave just the little one on.

There.

Now I'm going to tell you a story. It's a long one: there are ten nights in it, one at a time, not all of them tonight.

It's about a firefly.

But first I have to tell you one thing, and then I'll start.

When I was your age I couldn't sleep in the dark.

That isn't a figure of speech: I really couldn't. I kept the lamp on all night, and I kept it on even when they told me there was nothing there, that everything was fine, that I could turn it off.

We lived in a house at the top of a hill. Down at the bottom, at the foot of the hill, there was a meadow.

For the first few months I never saw that meadow. Because at night, with the lamp on, what you see from the window isn't the meadow. It's the window.

Then one evening a little gold dot settled on the glass.

It was tiny. And it was breathing: lighting up and going out, slowly, the way a living thing does when it's in no hurry at all.

I looked at it for a very long time.

And then I did the thing I had never done in my life: I reached out my hand and turned off the lamp.

And the meadow, down at the bottom, lit up all at once.

I can't explain it properly. It looked like a city seen from a plane, but alive, and all of it gold, and without a sound.

It had always been there. Every night, all along, while I sat on my side of the glass with the light on, seeing nothing.

All it took was a bit of dark.

Much later — when my legs had grown long — I opened the front door one evening and went down the hill to see it close up. I had a torch with me, and with the torch I found nothing at all: only grass, and stones, and my own white light banging about everywhere.

So I turned that off too, and waited in the wet grass without moving.

It took a while. It's the hardest thing in the world, keeping still.

Then one firefly lit up, two steps away from me. Then another. Then the whole meadow.

And one of them settled on my open hand, and stayed there until they called me in from the house.

So: the stories I'm going to tell you from here on, I know them because that yellow window was mine.

Now the house is this one, and the window is yours, and the person tucking you in is me.

When you're grown up, if you ever get the chance, take someone out to a meadow in the dark yourself.

Now close your eyes, because the story is starting.

It starts like this:

*Once upon a time, in a meadow at the foot of a hill, there was a little firefly called Nina.*
