# The firefly and the tired lantern

*A bedtime story — Book Two, following "The firefly who was afraid of the dark"*

---

A whole summer went by, and in the meadow at the foot of the hill everybody now knew the story of Nina, the firefly who had learned to light up.

Every night she flew from one end of the meadow to the other. She lit the path for the ants coming home late. She made light for the old mole who could never find her own front door. She saw home the lost snails, the muddled moths, the dandelion seeds that had lost the wind.

And the cricket — who had a name now, he was called Rocco — watched her go past from his little house under the root of the oak.

«Nina,» he said to her one evening, «do you ever rest?»

«I can't,» she answered, already flying off. «Somebody might need me.»

But then September came.

The nights grew longer and colder. The brook slowed down. The flowers closed up and didn't open again.

And one night something happened that frightened Nina more than all the dark in the world.

Her lantern went out.

Not slowly, the way it does when a firefly breathes. It just went out, like a candle in the wind.

Nina landed, shaking, on a stone by the brook.

«Oh no,» she whispered. «Oh no, oh no.»

She tried to light up again. She screwed her eyes shut, held her breath, put everything she had into it.

Nothing. Just grey.

«There,» she wept. «Now I'm no use to anyone.»

That was when she heard a small *frin frin* in the grass.

It was Rocco, climbing up the stone on his little legs, puffing a bit, because it was quite a climb for a cricket.

«I saw you from down there,» he said.

«But… how? I'm out.»

Rocco sat down next to her.

«Nina,» he said gently, «I never looked for your light. I looked for *you*.»

Nina didn't know what to say.

«Do you know why your lantern went out?» the cricket went on. «Not because it's broken. Because it's tired. You've lit this whole meadow all summer long and you haven't stopped for a single night.»

«But if I stop, the dark comes back.»

«The dark comes back anyway,» said Rocco. «It comes back every evening. It isn't your fault and it isn't your job. You're a firefly, not the sun.»

Nina sat quietly for a long time, listening to the water.

«So what do I do?»

«Now?» Rocco lifted his antennae towards the sky. «Now we look at the stars. And tomorrow you sleep. And the day after that you sleep some more, if you like.»

«And if someone gets lost?»

«Then I'll sing,» said the cricket. «I can't make light. But I can make a noise, and in the dark a voice is sometimes all you need to know which way to go.»

And so, for the first time in months, Nina didn't fly.

She stayed on the stone, beside her friend, and together they looked at the September sky, which was full, full, full of stars — the stars she had never had time to look at, because she was always too busy making light.

And while Rocco sang quietly in the cold grass, Nina fell asleep.

She slept all autumn. She slept all winter, curled up in the warm heart of the old oak, and the cricket slept nearby, and the snow covered the meadow like a white blanket.

Then one day the air turned soft, the brook started singing again, and the first clover came up out of the ground.

Nina opened her eyes.

And with no effort, no fear, without even thinking about it —

she lit up.

Brighter than before.

Because even the loveliest lights need to rest, my love.

Yours too.

And now close your eyes: the meadow is safe, the cricket is on watch, and tomorrow there will be light again.

Goodnight. 🌙✨
