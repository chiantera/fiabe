# Prologue

*Pia's stories — to be read before all the others*

---

I told you the firefly's story because I saw it with my own eyes.

The mole's, on trust. The bat's I heard, every night, through the ceiling.

This one I found.

A little piece at a time, on the ground, among the roots of the old oak at the far end of the meadow.

I have to confess something: when I was your age, I lost everything. Marbles, buttons, pencils, the cap of my water bottle, a key I never found out the use of. I lost them mostly in the meadow, and I lost them for good. Or so I thought.

Then one afternoon, among the roots of the oak, on the sunny side, I found my blue marble.

I hadn't lost it there. I'd lost it by the stream, months before, and the stream is a long way from the oak. But it was mine: it had the same little bubble inside, crooked.

Next to the marble there was a tiny button, with a blade of grass threaded through its holes. There was a thimble full of earth, and in the earth a sprout. And there was a scrap of bark with something written on it, in writing so small that it took me a week, with a magnifying glass, to read it.

It said: *You'll find everything here. Sooner or later.*

I didn't tell anybody. I already knew what they'd say: that it was the ants, that it was the wind, that I should go and play.

But I started leaving things there, on purpose. A button. A bead. A stub of pencil.

The next morning they were gone.

And in their place, nearly always, there was an acorn. Just one, set down neatly, like the change they give you in a shop.

I kept that up for years. I still have the acorns, in a tin box.

I never saw who lived down there. What I'm going to tell you I put together from what I found, the way you do with a jigsaw when the box is long gone. Some pieces I had to imagine. But the picture, I promise you, is the right one.

Before we start, just one thing.

If you lose something tonight, don't worry too much.

Maybe it isn't lost. Maybe it's just rolling.

This is her story.

It begins like this:

*Among the roots of the old oak, at the far end of the meadow, there was a shop where you could find everything.*
